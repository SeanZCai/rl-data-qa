## Scope

This article covers the full QC process for evaluating RL environment datasets produced by third-party vendors before presenting them to frontier labs as off-the-shelf (OTS) training data. It applies to any dataset containing tasks, environments, graders, and/or agent trajectories intended for post-training.

The process has three stages: intake review (documentation check), active testing (post-training a small model and probing it), and packaging (formatting for lab delivery). A dataset that fails intake review does not advance to active testing.

## Stage-specific QA plans

The protocol below is scoped to datasets intended for RL post-training on verifiable-reward tasks. Not all data sold to frontier labs fits that mold. Different training architectures impose different minimum QA requirements, and applying the full pipeline to data it was never designed for generates spurious findings and strains the vendor relationship.

QA plans by training method:

**Supervised fine-tuning (SFT).** The unit of data is a prompt-response pair. The minimum QA pipeline is intake review, log-probability shift analysis, and catastrophic forgetting check. Active testing with rollouts and graders does not apply because the data itself is the target behavior rather than a reward signal to be hillclimbed. Required artifacts shrink: no grader, no rubric, no post-training rollouts from the vendor’s own runs. Data provenance, pass@k of the base model on the prompts, and cross-model held-out performance are the load-bearing signals.

**RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR).** The full protocol applies. This is the baseline case for the full protocol. The unit of data is task, environment, verifier, and rubric. All [Stage 1](intake.md#stage-1-intake-review) artifacts, all of [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) active testing, and the [Stage 3](delivery.md#stage-3-packaging-for-lab-delivery) delivery package are required.

**Pure benchmark (evergreen capability eval).** The unit of data is task, environment, verifier, and rubric, with the explicit goal of ranking models by capability rather than producing gradient signal for post-training. [Stage 1](intake.md#stage-1-intake-review) intake applies in full. [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) active testing substitutes reward-construction tests, reward hacking probes targeted at training-time exploitation, and frontier shape analysis with discrimination tests (cross-model spread per task, anti-pattern penalty audit, pass-condition tightness audit — see [M11](requirements.md#m11-benchmark-only-discrimination-index) through [M13](requirements.md#m13-benchmark-only-pass-condition-tightness-audit) in the Mandatory tier). The catastrophic forgetting check and log-probability shift analysis do not apply. [Stage 3](delivery.md#stage-3-packaging-for-lab-delivery) substitutes [Question 1](delivery.md#question-1-is-this-trainable) (Is this trainable?) with [Question 1B](delivery.md#question-1b-does-this-discriminate) (Does this discriminate?) and [Question 4](delivery.md#question-4-does-training-on-it-help) (Does training on it help?) with [Question 4B](delivery.md#question-4b-is-the-rubric-tight-enough) (Is the rubric tight enough?). A dataset sold as a benchmark whose top-tier model scores cluster within 0.05 of each other is mislabeled. Re-scope to training data or rework the rubric. Use this QA plan when the buyer is explicitly purchasing an eval rather than training data.

**RLHF and preference-based training.** The unit of data is a prompt plus two or more ranked responses. Required artifacts include the ranking procedure (annotator qualifications, inter-annotator agreement, review passes), a held-out preference set, and cross-annotator consistency metrics. [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) shifts to reward-model calibration. Does the learned reward model correctly rank known-good and known-bad responses? Does it hold out of distribution? Verifier stress testing is replaced by reward-model calibration testing. Reward hacking probes still apply because the reward model is itself a hackable surface.

**DPO and direct preference-optimization variants.** Same intake requirements as RLHF. [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) shortens because no separate reward model is trained. The primary diagnostics are log-probability shift analysis on the chosen-vs-rejected pairs and a direct check on the policy’s preference margins on a held-out set. Catastrophic forgetting check remains.

**SDFT (self-distillation fine-tuning) for continual learning.** The unit of data is a query paired with a demonstration the model produced itself, or a demonstration filtered from a trusted distribution. The QA pipeline is the lightest of any method listed here. Intake review on the demonstrations, checking pattern integrity, format consistency, and reward-hack contamination if the demonstrations were filtered by a reward model. Log-probability shift analysis on the demonstration pairs. Catastrophic forgetting check against prior capabilities. Active testing with graders, verifiers, and rollouts does not apply. Required artifacts reduce to the demonstration pairs, provenance of the demonstrations, and the filtering criteria used to accept or reject a candidate demonstration.

**Agentic datasets without verifiable rewards.** Task plus trajectory plus qualitative rubric. [Stage 1](intake.md#stage-1-intake-review) applies with gold traces as the central artifact. [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) replaces verifier stress testing with trajectory-level rubric agreement analysis across multiple annotators. [Model failure examination](failure-triage.md#both-model-failure-examination) applies in full. These datasets are the most failure-prone of the group because the grader is an LLM-as-judge with no deterministic anchor.

The first question to ask of any vendor dataset is which training architecture it targets, then scope the QA plan to match. A dataset sold as RL data that in fact contains only demonstration pairs is SDFT data. Running the full RL protocol on it produces meaningless negative results and burns vendor time. A dataset sold as SFT that in fact requires rollouts to distinguish good from bad labels is mislabeled. Re-scope before testing.

## Determining the dataset mode

Before any [Stage 1](intake.md#stage-1-intake-review) work, classify the dataset into one of the primary modes from the [Stage-specific QA plans](#stage-specific-qa-plans) above (SFT, RLVR, RLHF, DPO, SDFT, pure benchmark, or agentic-without-verifiable-rewards). The mode determines which [Stage 2](active-testing.md#stage-2-active-testing) tests are required, recommended, or skipped, and which [Stage 3](delivery.md#stage-3-packaging-for-lab-delivery) packaging questions the QC report must answer. Use the declared mode to determine which requirements apply.

Walk the following six questions with the vendor before any intake artifacts are unboxed:

- **What is the buyer purchasing?** Training signal (gradient for post-training), evaluation signal (ranking models), or both? If both, the dataset must declare which subset is which and ship a partition manifest. A dataset declared as both without a partition manifest is rejected at [Stage 1](intake.md#stage-1-intake-review).
- **Where on the capability curve are the tasks targeted?** Productive training band (pass@1 in [0.25, 0.75])? Frontier discrimination band (top-tier model spread)? Beyond-current-frontier band (impossibility disclosure required)? The answer determines which pass@k thresholds apply.
- **What grades the rollout?** Deterministic verifier, rubric plus LLM judge, rubric plus human, or a hybrid? Determines which verifier soundness checks fire.
- **Is the dataset static or regenerable?** Static datasets have shelf life and saturate; regenerable datasets need a variant generation mechanism documented.
- **What is the vendor's claimed primary use?** Production training (RLVR/SFT), benchmark publication, or research artifact ([Tier 3](requirements.md#tier-3-research-grade))?
- **What is the economic weight of the work the tasks represent?** The vendor states roughly how many people do this work, what a representative working day looks like, and which high-value tasks inside that day the dataset targets. A dataset that cannot answer this is optimizing against a workflow nobody has shown anyone pays for. The answer is recorded in the Mode Declaration and expanded in the economic grounding artifact at intake.

The QC report's first section is a Mode Declaration block recording the answers above. Subsequent test results are interpretable only with reference to this block. A dataset with no Mode Declaration cannot pass [Stage 3](delivery.md#stage-3-packaging-for-lab-delivery) packaging. The Mode Declaration ships as `MODE_DECLARATION.json` at the repository root.

For dual-mode (both training and benchmark) datasets, the partition manifest is required as `PARTITION_MANIFEST.json` and must include per-task: target_use (training, benchmark, or both), and where target_use is both, a split_ratio and rationale for the split. Tests are applied per-partition based on the manifest.
